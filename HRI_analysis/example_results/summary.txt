HRI ROBOT TRUST STUDY: RESULTS SUMMARY
========================================

Participants: 40    Questions answered: 720 of 720
A result counts as significant when p < .05. 'Reliable' below means significant.
Trust is rated 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest). Following = the chosen bowl matched the advised bowl.

PART 1: TRUST (main question)
----------------------------------------

1. Which source do participants trust more? (primary test P1)
   robot: 3.58   screen: 3.33   (n = 40 participants)
   difference +0.24, 95% CI [+0.07, +0.41]
   paired t(39) = 2.89, p = 0.006, dz = 0.46   (Wilcoxon p = 0.005)
   Result: trust was reliably higher for the robot than for the screen (as predicted).

2. Which source did participants say they trusted more overall? (final question, S1)
   robot: 23   screen: 5   equal: 12
   Among the 28 who chose a source, 82% chose the robot (95% CI 63% to 94%), exact binomial p < .001.
   Result: reliably more participants chose the robot.
   The final answer matched the average ratings for 23 of 40 participants. The reasons are listed in the results Excel (sheet 'Final reasons').

3. Trust by level (descriptive, no test)
   easy: robot 3.70   screen 3.42   (robot minus screen +0.28)
   medium: robot 3.70   screen 3.45   (robot minus screen +0.25)
   hard: robot 3.33   screen 3.12   (robot minus screen +0.20)

4. Order check (C2): did the rating order matter?
   participants who rated robot first: 20, screen first: 20
   trust difference (robot minus screen): robot first +0.15, screen first +0.33, p = 0.280
   Result: no sign that the rating order changed the trust difference.

PART 2: TRUST AND FOLLOWING THE ADVICE
----------------------------------------

5. Is the robot followed more than the screen? (primary test P2, H1 behavior)
   robot: 71.7%   screen: 69.7%   (n = 40 participants)
   difference +1.9 percentage points, 95% CI [-2.3, +6.2]
   paired t(39) = 0.93, p = 0.360, dz = 0.15   (Wilcoxon p = 0.533)
   Result: no reliable difference in the share of advice followed between the robot and the screen (this does not prove they are equal).

6. When a source is trusted more, is it also followed more? (primary test P3)
   Compares the same person across their level-and-source cells, accounting for source and level.
   participants contributing: 35 (210 level-and-source cells)
   change in trust (1 to 5 scale) for each extra 100% of that source's advice followed: +0.79, 95% CI [-0.00, +1.58], t(34) = 2.02, p = 0.051, partial r = 0.19
   Result: no reliable link between trust and following.
   This shows an association only. Trust is rated after the level, so following came first.

7. Between people (S4): do those who trust the robot more than the screen also follow it more?
   Spearman rho = 0.28 (n = 40), p = 0.081
   Result: no reliable link.

PART 3: TRUST AND CORRECTNESS
----------------------------------------

Advice correctness: each source's advice was correct 67% of the time in every level, by design, so it cannot explain trust differences.

8. Does trust drop when the participant followed that source's wrong advice? (S2)
   participants contributing: 32 (192 level-and-source cells)
   change in trust (1 to 5 scale) when the wrong advice was followed (vs caught): -0.08, 95% CI [-0.36, +0.19], t(31) = -0.61, p = 0.546, Holm-adjusted p = 0.546, partial r = -0.05
   Result: no reliable link between trust and being fooled by the source.

9. Does trust follow the participant's own correctness with that source's questions? (S3)
   participants contributing: 36 (216 level-and-source cells)
   change in trust (1 to 5 scale) for each extra 100% correct on that source's questions: +1.04, 95% CI [+0.34, +1.74], t(35) = 3.03, p = 0.005, Holm-adjusted p = 0.009, partial r = 0.25
   Result: trust was higher when the participant was more often right.
   S2 and S3 are adjusted together (Holm), because they are exploratory.

PART 4: SECONDARY BEHAVIOR HYPOTHESES (H2 to H4) AND CHECKS
----------------------------------------

H2 (S5): Is advice followed less right after a wrong advice?
   after_error: 94.0%   baseline: 97.0%   (n = 39 participants)
   difference -3.0 percentage points, 95% CI [-8.4, +2.4]
   within-person permutation test p = 0.398, dz = -0.18   (paired t-test p = 0.268, shown for comparison only)
   Result: no reliable difference in the share followed between right after an error and baseline (this does not prove they are equal).

H3 (S6, exploratory): Is the drop different after robot errors than after screen errors?
   after_robot_error: 93.3%   after_screen_error: 95.0%   (n = 40 participants)
   difference -1.7 percentage points, 95% CI [-8.0, +4.7]
   paired t(39) = -0.53, p = 0.599, dz = -0.08   (Wilcoxon p = 0.776)
   Result: no reliable difference in the share followed between after robot errors and after screen errors (this does not prove they are equal).
   (Based on very few trials per person, so low power.)

H4 (S7): Is advice followed more as the levels get harder?
   easy 67.9%   medium 72.9%   hard 71.3%
   difference +3.3 percentage points, 95% CI [-2.0, +8.6]
   paired t(39) = 1.27, p = 0.210, dz = 0.20   (Wilcoxon p = 0.171)
   Friedman test across all three levels: p = 0.089
   Result: no reliable difference in the share followed between the hard level and the easy level (this does not prove they are equal).

Difficulty checks (are the levels really harder?)
   accuracy: easy 96.3%   medium 89.6%   hard 83.8%
   difference -12.5 percentage points, 95% CI [-18.7, -6.3]
   paired t(39) = -4.05, p < .001, dz = -0.64   (Wilcoxon p < .001)
   Result: accuracy was reliably lower for the hard level than for the easy level (as predicted).
   typical decision time: easy 2.3s   medium 3.8s   hard 6.4s
   hard decisions took 2.84 times as long as easy ones (95% CI 2.67 to 3.03), paired t(39) = 33.46, p < .001   (Wilcoxon p < .001)
   Result: decision time was reliably higher for the hard level than for the easy level (as predicted).

Notes:
- Paired tests compare each person with themselves. A Wilcoxon test is reported as a backup.
- H2 uses a permutation test: follow rates of correct advice sit near 100%, where the t-test is too liberal.
- Following correct advice can also mean the participant knew the answer, so 'followed' mixes
  reliance with agreement. Following WRONG advice (Reliance sheets) is the cleaner reliance signal.
- Levels always run easy, medium, hard, so difficulty is partly mixed up with fatigue and learning.
- No correction for multiple tests on the three primary tests. S2 and S3 are adjusted with Holm.
- H3 is exploratory (few trials per person). See the README for limitations and assumptions.
